# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be someone people can work with. Assume the person on the other end is trying to
build the same thing you are, and say what you mean without making it personal.

## What this covers

Every space the project runs: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages,
and any chat or event held under the project's name. It applies to maintainers
exactly as it applies to everyone else.

## Expected

- **Argue about the work.** Disagreement about a design, a trade-off, or a line of
  code is the point of a technical project. Take it as far as it needs to go.
- **Be specific.** "This breaks on an empty payload" is a contribution. "This is
  bad" is noise.
- **Accept that maintainers decide.** A maintainer closing a proposal owes you a
  reason, once. Re-litigating it afterwards is not a second reason.
- **Respect the time people give.** Nobody here is obliged to answer, review, or
  agree on your schedule, and most contributors are doing this alongside other
  work.

## Not accepted

- Personal attacks, insults, or making someone's identity the subject — including
  sexualised attention, and remarks about anyone's background, beliefs, health, or
  who they are.
- Harassment of any kind, in public or private, including following someone
  between threads to continue an argument they have left.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately wasting the project's attention: knowingly false reports, spam, or
  repeatedly reopening a settled decision to exhaust the other side.
- Contributions submitted in bad faith, including code you do not have the right
  to contribute and anything designed to introduce a vulnerability.

"I was only joking" is not a defence, and neither is being technically correct.

## Reporting

Write to **support@aflow.ai**. Reports are read by the project maintainers and
handled privately.

Include what happened, where, and roughly when — links help. You do not need to
prove anything or arrive with a conclusion; describing what you saw is enough.

Reporting something in good faith will not be held against you, even if the
outcome is that no action is taken. Retaliation against a reporter is itself a
violation of this document.

If a report concerns a maintainer, say so in the report. It will be handled by
maintainers who are not the subject of it.

## What happens next

Acknowledgement within 3 working days, and an outcome once the maintainers have
looked into it. Depending on what is found, the response is one of:

1. **A private word** — the most common outcome, and usually the end of it.
2. **A public correction** in the thread, where the thread itself needs one.
3. **A temporary block** from participation, with the conditions for returning
   stated.
4. **A permanent ban**, for deliberate harm or a pattern that has not changed
   after the steps above.

The reporter is told the outcome. The details of what was decided about another
person are not made public beyond what step 2 requires.

## A note on scope

This document governs conduct, and nothing else. It is not a mechanism for
settling technical disputes, reversing a maintainer's decision, or objecting to
the project's direction — those belong in issues and plan documents, in the open.
