# Trademark and brand-use policy

"Aflow" and the Aflow logo are trademarks of Karim Elmansi. The AGPL grants broad
rights over the _software_; it grants none over the _name_. This document says what
the project permits anyway, so that nobody has to ask for the ordinary cases.

## Why a policy at all

A copyleft licence lets anyone modify and redistribute this software, which is the
point. The consequence is that a modified version can behave differently, hold
different data, or reach different places — and a user who installed something
called "Aflow" has no way to know which they are running. The mark is what keeps
that distinguishable. It protects the people running the software rather than the
person holding the mark.

## Use that needs no permission

- **Saying what your software is.** "Built with Aflow", "compatible with Aflow",
  "an Aflow skill", "runs on Aflow" — nominative use, in plain text, describing an
  accurate relationship.
- **Writing about the project.** Articles, talks, tutorials, comparisons, reviews,
  and criticism. Favourable or not.
- **Unmodified redistribution.** Passing on an unmodified release, with its name
  and marks intact, including mirroring images or packages.
- **Community content.** Naming a skill, applet, connector or template you publish
  for Aflow, provided the name makes clear it is yours: `postgres-backup for
Aflow`, not `Aflow Postgres`.
- **Internal use.** Anything inside your own organisation, including a modified
  build, as long as it is not offered to the public under the mark.

Nothing above requires asking, and nothing above is revocable while it stays
accurate.

## Use that needs permission

- **Naming a modified version "Aflow"**, or a name that reads as one — including
  `Aflow Pro`, `Aflow Cloud`, `Aflow Enterprise`, `Aflow+`, or a translation.
- **A service offered to the public under the mark.** Running a modified Aflow as
  a hosted product called Aflow, or a substring of it.
- **Domains, social accounts, and app-store listings** whose name is or contains
  "aflow" in a way that implies the project runs them.
- **The logo**, other than reproducing it unchanged to refer to the project.
- **Anything implying endorsement, affiliation, certification, or partnership.**

Ask at **support@aflow.ai**. Permission for a specific use is usually
straightforward; the answer is slow only when the use would be hard to tell apart
from the project itself.

## What a fork should do

Forking is expressly welcome — it is a right the licence grants and this document
does not narrow it. What the mark asks is that a fork be findable as a fork:

1. **Pick your own name.** It may reference Aflow descriptively — "Foo, a fork of
   Aflow" — without being built from it.
2. **Replace the logo**, and say in your README what you changed.
3. **Point bug reports at yourself.** A fork's users reaching this project's issue
   tracker is the concrete harm this policy exists to prevent.
4. **Do not use the project's domains, accounts, or support channels** as if they
   were yours.

## Using the marks correctly

- Write it **Aflow** — one capital, no camel case, not all caps, never pluralised
  or used as a verb.
- Do not alter the logo's colours, proportions, or elements, and do not combine it
  with other marks into a single lockup.
- Do not use the marks as your own product name, logo, or favicon.

## Limits of this document

This is a permissions grant, not a transfer, and it can be updated — a change
applies to new uses rather than retroactively withdrawing a use that was permitted
when it began. It says nothing about patents, and nothing here overrides the
software licence in LICENSE.

Trademark law varies by jurisdiction and this document is not legal advice. It has
not yet been reviewed by counsel; until it has, treat it as the project's stated
intent, and ask if a planned use is unclear.
